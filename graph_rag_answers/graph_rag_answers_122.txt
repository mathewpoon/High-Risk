### Treatment with Vitamin B-12 Injections

Vitamin B-12 injections are commonly used to treat **pernicious anemia**. Pernicious anemia is a condition characterized by the inability to absorb vitamin B-12 from the gastrointestinal tract due to a lack of intrinsic factor, a protein necessary for vitamin B-12 absorption. This condition leads to chronic anemia and can predispose individuals to other health issues, such as carcinoma of the stomach [Data: Entities (3083); Sources (1205)].

### Explanation of Other Conditions

- **Bell's Palsy**: This condition involves sudden weakness or paralysis of the facial muscles, typically on one side of the face. It is not treated with vitamin B-12 injections.

- **Crohn's Disease**: This is an inflammatory bowel disease that affects the gastrointestinal tract, causing symptoms like abdominal pain and diarrhea. While nutritional deficiencies can occur in Crohn's disease, vitamin B-12 injections are not a standard treatment unless a specific deficiency is identified [Data: Entities (5121); Relationships (7752, 10558); Sources (2415)].

- **Graves' Disease**: This is an autoimmune disorder that leads to overactivity of the thyroid gland (hyperthyroidism). It is not treated with vitamin B-12 injections, but rather with medications that manage thyroid hormone levels [Data: Sources (2649)].

In summary, among the options provided, pernicious anemia is the condition that is treated with vitamin B-12 injections.
